Otolith Archives of Change: Ecotype Dynamics and Climate Stress in Baltic Herring and Cod

Project Info

Name Description
Title Otolith Archives of Change: Ecotype Dynamics and Climate Stress in Baltic Herring and Cod
Programme August T. Larsson Guest Researcher Programme
Funding Entity Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (SLU), August T. Larsson Foundation
Period 2026–2028
Guest Researcher Dr. Joana Vasconcelos (IU-ECOAQUA, ULPGC)
Host Researcher Dr. Yvette Heimbrand (SLU Aqua)
Host Institution Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (SLU), Department of Aquatic Resources, SLU Aqua
Amount funded 638 844 SEK
Research visits Up to two months per year during three years

Aim

The project aims to use historical otolith archives to investigate how ecotype structure and climate-related environmental stress shape morphological variation in Baltic Sea fishes. Focusing on Atlantic herring (Clupea harengus) and Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua), the project will apply advanced otolith contour shape analysis to improve discrimination among ecotypes and morphotypes and to identify temporal morphological changes associated with environmental variability, particularly hypoxia, temperature, salinity and fisheries pressure.

Research framework

The project combines SLU's extensive historical otolith collections and long-term environmental monitoring datasets with wavelet-based approaches. Cartesian wavelet analyses will be used to quantify biologically informative features of otolith shape and distinguish known herring ecotypes, including spring- and autumn-spawning components, as well as cod morphotypes. Shape variation will then be integrated with environmental time series to assess whether observed morphological patterns reflect persistent ecotype structure or temporal plasticity associated with climate and ecosystem change.

Aim

The main purpose of the present proposal is to explore complex life cycles in metacommunities to predict future scenarios in human-exploited ecosystems.

Background

Human sprawl is all over the globe, affecting most species on Earth. Ecosystems are becoming increasingly fragmented, causing habitat loss and species ranges reduction through shifts on the structure, diversity, dynamics, species composition and recruitment rates of communities. Most metacommunity studies have explored biodiversity dynamics considering simple lifecycles. Yet, the effects of habitat loss and exploitation on complex life cycles species in metacommunities are currently unknown.

Research plan

We propose a project combining experiments, database analysis and modeling from intertidal metacommunities as a case study to explore future trajectories in fragmented and exploited ecosystems accounting for spatial structure, complex life cycles, migration and demographic processes. This will be achieved by focusing on three main objectives: (1) Infer how complex life cycles are influencing persistence trajectories along disturbed metacommunities; (2) Interpreting the role of human-exploitation on spatial synchrony and extinction probability; and (3) Explore diversity patterns at intra-and inter-specific level to disentangle the role of the spatial structure in predicting metacommunity persistence.

Project Tasks

  • Task 1: Project Management
  • Task 2: Fecundity Estimation
  • Task 3: Data Standardization
  • Task 4: Formulation of the Model Structure and Implementation of the Model in Open Software
  • Task 5: Parametrisation and Simulations of the Different Scencarios of the Modelling in a Workstation
  • Task 6: Model-Data-Comparisons using Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC) Methods
  • Task 7: Generation of Jupiter Notebook to Facilitate Reproducibility of the Whole Modelling and Model-Data Cycle
  • Task 8: Outreach and Dissemination Actions
